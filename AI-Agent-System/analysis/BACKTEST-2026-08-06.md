## The headline is not the average — it is the spread

**47 percentage points** separate the best quote from the worst. That is not a pricing
policy set slightly too low; it is the absence of a pricing method. The same person, the
same rate card, three weeks apart, produced +46.5% and −0.3%.

An average of 15.8% would suggest "raise prices a bit." The spread says something different:
**the number is being set by feel, and the feel is uncalibrated.**

## Why Mecca went wrong — cost is driven by equipment, not room size

| | Samoosa | Mecca |
|---|---|---|
| Rooms | 1 | 2 |
| Panel area | 60.09 sqm | **57.04 sqm** (less) |
| Equipment sets | 1 | **2** |
| Condensing + evaporator | 6,400 | **15,200** |
| Control panels · pipe · wiring | 5,500 | **11,000** |
| Doors | 1,800 | 3,600 |
| **Total cost** | **26,283** | **46,131** |
| Quoted | 38,500 | 46,000 |

Mecca has **less panel than Samoosa** but **75% more cost**, because a second room means a
second condensing unit, a second evaporator, a second control panel, a second pipe run and a
second wiring run. Panel area is the visible part of the job and the smallest part of the bill.

Mecca was priced 19.5% above Samoosa. It costs 75% more.

**This is the actual failure mode.** Not "quotes too low" — *quotes scaled to how big the job
looks rather than how much equipment it needs.* Two-room jobs are where the money leaks, and
small two-room jobs are the worst case.
