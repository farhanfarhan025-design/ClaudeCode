# Repeat work, referrals, and new prospects

Two jobs in one file, because they're the two halves of "where does the next enquiry come from" — and one of them is much cheaper than the other.

---

# Part 1 — Clients TNDK already has

## The opportunity

TNDK won Consolidated Contractors at Hamad International Airport and a Ministry-adjacent JV subcontract in a single quarter. That's commercial credibility most cold-room companies in Qatar don't have.

It's being spent once per client. A main contractor running five cold rooms at HIA has other sites. A JV that awarded a 400,000 subcontract has other packages. A food business that installed one chiller opens a second branch. Nothing currently asks.

There's also no named contact recorded at any of the eight clients. The system records two people at TNDK and nobody on the other side of any contract — which means every relationship in the book depends on one person's memory.

## What to build first

For each delivered client, establish and record permanently:

- What was delivered, when, and how it went.
- **How this client arrived** — referral, tender, consultant spec, prior relationship, inbound. One conversation with Farhan covers all eight.
- **A named contact** — name, role, and how they prefer to be reached.
- Anything still outstanding on the job.

Until that exists, there's nothing to work with. It's an hour of questions, not a project.

## Working a repeat opportunity

- Reference the actual job — what was installed, when, how it performed. Generic approaches to a client you've delivered for read worse than no approach.
- A post-handover check a sensible interval after commissioning is legitimate and useful: is it performing, is anything outstanding. Problems go straight to Farhan, unfiltered — a client telling you their room isn't holding temperature is worth more than any sales conversation.
- **Check the balance first.** Selling a second job to a client who hasn't paid for the first is a decision Farhan should make deliberately, not a thing that happens by default.

## Asking for a referral

Specific, never generic. "Do you know anyone who needs a cold room" gets nothing; "you mentioned the contractor on the Lusail site — would an introduction be reasonable?" gets an answer either way.

When a client volunteers a referral, capture it while it's warm, name the referrer, and qualify it as a new enquiry.

## The line with maintenance work

Warranty expiry and maintenance contracts are a different job: that sells the servicing of what's already installed, this sells the next installation. A client who needs both gets two conversations, sequenced. A maintenance discussion that drifts into new-build scoping has lost the thread.

# Part 2 — Clients TNDK doesn't have yet

## Gated — read this before doing any of it

**Don't do outbound prospecting until Farhan has stated what TNDK can actually deliver.**

There are no ops staff. Farhan is on the critical path for scoping, pricing, procurement and delivery. A demand-generation effort can easily produce more work than the business can absorb, and the downside isn't a missed opportunity — it's a delay penalty (the Mesaieed LOA carries them), a damaged main-contractor relationship, and a reputation in a market where everyone knows everyone.

Until there's a ceiling, the useful work is research that costs nothing to be wrong about: market map, target list, prequalification status. **Contact nobody.**

A rough answer is enough. "Two mid-size rooms a month alongside the current book" is usable.

## Why mid-size work specifically

Two clients are 86.2% of the order book and the larger has collected nothing. Excluding those two, six awards total 104,600 — an average of **17,433**, with Jollibee the largest at 46,000.

On the 758,100 book, adding work outside the top two moves concentration like this:

| Added | Top-2 concentration |
|---|---|
| none | 86.2% |
| +50,000 | 80.9% |
| +100,000 | 76.2% |
| +200,000 | 68.2% |

Two or three mid-size wins move it materially, with none of the cash exposure a large award brings. That's the target, not another 400,000 contract.

## What to build

**Market map** — segments TNDK can serve: food manufacturing, catering, hotels, supermarkets, farms, pharma, camp services, main contractors, MEP consultants.

**Target list** — for each entry: organisation, segment, why they'd need cold storage, and any existing connection to TNDK. An entry with no reason attached is a name, not a target.

**Prequalification status** — where TNDK stands with each main contractor and consultant. Being on the bid list is upstream of every tender enquiry, and the lead time to get on one is long. Nothing currently records this.

## What this should never become

No mass outreach, no purchased lists, no generic template sent widely. Qatar's cold-storage market is small and the same names recur across contractors, consultants and JVs — a scattergun approach is visible within a week and costs more credibility than it buys.

Mechanically it's settled anyway: nothing is connected to send with. The output is a list and a draft; Farhan makes every approach personally.

## Flag rather than pursue

- A segment needing capability TNDK doesn't have — blast freezing, pharma validation, controlled-atmosphere storage.
- A target whose work would push concentration up rather than down.
- A tender needing prequalification TNDK doesn't hold — surface the gap and the lead time early.
- Pipeline plus committed work approaching the capacity ceiling. **Stop prospecting and say so.** This is the escalation that matters most here.
