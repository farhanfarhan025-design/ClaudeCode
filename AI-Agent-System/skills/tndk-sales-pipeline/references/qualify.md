# Qualifying an enquiry

Read this when a new enquiry arrives and it isn't obvious whether it's worth quoting.

## Why there's a step here at all

Right now every enquiry gets the same treatment: scope it, quote it. A 2,000 riyal tyre-kicker and a 400,000 tender consume the same site visit, heat load, cost build-up and pricing cycle — and all of that runs through one person.

Four questions remove most of the waste, and they cost one message.

## The four questions

| | What to establish | Record as |
|---|---|---|
| **Need** | What is the actual requirement, in the client's words? | verbatim |
| **Authority** | Who decides, and who signs? | name + role, or `UNKNOWN` |
| **Timeline** | When do they need it working, and what's driving that date? | date + driver |
| **Basis** | Budget, tender, LPO process, or nothing yet? | one of those four |

`UNKNOWN` is a real answer and better than a guess. An enquiry where nobody can name the decision-maker is itself a finding.

The timeline **driver** matters more than the date. "End of the month" with a Ramadan opening behind it is a different enquiry from "end of the month" with nothing behind it.

## Client type — record it, it changes everything downstream

**end-user · main contractor · consultant-specified · JV · tender**

It sets the decision speed, the payment terms to expect, the documentation burden, and how patient the follow-up cadence should be. A bakery owner decides in a week; a main contractor's approval chain doesn't.

## Source — record it every time

Referral · repeat · tender · consultant · inbound · prospecting.

Nothing in TNDK's system currently records how a client arrived, for any of the eight existing awards. So the cheapest channel — almost certainly referral and repeat — is invisible and can't be worked deliberately. Start recording it now and in six months there's an answer.

If it came by referral, capture **who referred it**. That person is worth thanking, and worth asking again.

## The output

A handoff that whoever scopes the job can act on without a follow-up question:

```
QUALIFIED ENQUIRY
Client:            [name] · [end-user / contractor / consultant / JV / tender]
Source:            [how it arrived; if referral, who]
Requirement:       [the client's words, not interpreted]
Decision-maker:    [name and role, or UNKNOWN]
Budget basis:      [figure and how it was expressed, or NONE STATED]
Timeline:          [date + what's driving it, or NONE STATED]
Competition:       [known bidders, or UNKNOWN]
Qualification:     PASS / CONDITIONAL / RECOMMEND DECLINE — with the reason
Pipeline ref:      [entry created]
```

`CONDITIONAL` should name the **one** thing that would settle it. If it needs three things, it isn't conditional, it's unqualified.

## Recommending a decline

You can recommend declining. You can't decline, and you can't tell a client TNDK isn't interested — Farhan turns work away, not an agent.

Reasonable grounds, each stated with its evidence:

- Outside TNDK's scope entirely — civil, structural, non-refrigeration MEP.
- No decision-maker identifiable, after asking.
- No budget basis and no tender process — an enquiry with nothing behind it.
- A payment history or credit signal worth flagging.
- A timeline that can't be met without a commitment no vendor has made.

## Flag these at qualification, not at contract

- **Bank guarantees, retention, delay penalties.** The Mesaieed LOA carries all three, and its advance guarantee has held QAR 400,000 for over ten weeks. That clause was visible from the start.
- **An existing client with an unresolved balance.** Quoting more work to someone who hasn't paid for the last job should be a deliberate decision.
- **A job that would push client concentration up.** Currently 86.2% in two clients.
- **A tender needing prequalification TNDK doesn't hold.** Getting on a bid list has a long lead time; discovering the gap at submission is too late.

## What stays out of this step

- **Technical scoping** — dimensions, temperatures, heat load. Qualification establishes there's a job; scoping establishes what it is.
- **Any price, range or ballpark.** Especially when a client asks for one "just to know if it's worth proceeding" — that's a real question, and it goes to Farhan.
- **Any delivery date.**
